<p>The UK Working Holiday visa, officially known as the Youth Mobility Scheme (YMS), is an incredibly useful visa that allows young nationals aged 18 to under 35 from eligible countries to live, work, and gain experience in the UK for up to 2 years.</p>

<p>While it does not lead directly to settlement and cannot be extended, it can be switched to other visa categories such as the Skilled Worker Visa or Student Visa, making it an excellent stepping stone for long-term settlement in the UK. In this article, we will take a detailed look at the legal Do's and Don'ts once you enter the UK on a YMS visa.</p>

<img class="blog-body-image" alt="UK YMS Visa Do's and Don'ts" src="/images/imin2uk-yms-dos-and-donts.webp" width="auto" height="auto" loading="auto">

<h2>1. Employment and Job Searching: General Employment is Highly Flexible</h2>

<p>Unlike the UK Skilled Worker Visa, the YMS visa has virtually no restrictions on the type of occupation, nor does it require sponsorship from an employer.</p>

<p>You can work in both part-time and full-time roles in cafes, restaurants, and supermarkets around the High Street. Furthermore, you are entirely free to apply to companies—large or small—using your university major or skills to work as a software developer, web designer, office professional, etc., to build your career. Essentially, almost all forms of legal employment are permitted.</p>

<h2>2. The Absolute Don't: Cash Jobs Without an NI Number</h2>

<p>There is one crucial rule you must remember when working in the UK: <strong>any labor where wages are paid unofficially outside the Payroll system is illegal.</strong></p>

<p>To work legally in the UK, your very first step must be to obtain a National Insurance (NI) Number. Whether you are working part-time or full-time, your employer is legally required to report your NI number to HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) and pay taxes through the PAYE (Pay As You Earn) system.</p>

<p>Occasionally, small businesses may offer you a so-called 'Cash Job,' proposing to pay you immediately in cash without an NI number or contract. This is tax evasion and illegal labor. <strong>Illegal Cash Jobs leave you completely unprotected by UK law against issues like unpaid wages, and if caught, it can lead to devastating consequences such as visa cancellation.</strong> Before starting work, always ensure you have a contract and confirm that you are registered on their Payroll. It is also much safer to receive your salary via a Bank Transfer rather than in cash to maintain proper records.</p>

<h2>3. Occupation Restrictions: Professional Sports and Coaching Are Prohibited</h2>

<p>According to UK Immigration Rules, YMS visa holders cannot work as professional sports players or coaches for professional teams. Engaging in a professional league or developing professional athletes is strictly prohibited. If you wish to pursue professional sports activities, you must apply for a specific sports visa.</p>

<p>However, activities for Recreation or Education purposes are allowed. Working as a local swimming instructor, Pilates teacher, or Taekwondo assistant instructor is perfectly fine. If you are concerned about how it may look on your CV or documents, we recommend using titles like 'Instructor' or 'Tutor' rather than 'Coach'.</p>

<h2>4. Self-Employed and Freelancer Regulations</h2>

<p>As a rule, YMS visa holders are not allowed to operate formal businesses, such as running an offline retail store. However, small-scale self-employment in the form of freelancing is granted as an exception. If you become self-employed, you must adhere to the following conditions:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>No Ownership of Business Premises:</strong> You cannot take out a Commercial Lease for a shop, office, or factory under your name. You must work from your rented residential space or use a shared office (sharing a studio or workshop is permitted).</li>
<li><strong>Equipment Value Limit:</strong> The total value of any equipment or machinery used for your business must not exceed £5,000.</li>
<li><strong>No Hiring Employees:</strong> You cannot hire other employees to run your business; you must operate solely as a one-person freelancer.</li>
</ul>

<p>General translation work, tutoring from home, web development, online purchasing agency work, or running a small makeup/photography business using your own equipment is completely fine as long as the above conditions are met. When you start your Self-Employed activities, you must register for 'Self Assessment' with HMRC and receive a 'UTR' (Unique Taxpayer Reference) number.</p>

<h2>5. Study Regulations and Advantages</h2>

<p>One of the biggest advantages of the YMS visa is that there are almost no restrictions on studying.</p>

<p><strong>Short-Term Language Study:</strong> Normally, entering the UK on an 11-month Short-term Student visa completely prohibits you from working. However, if you enter on a YMS visa and register part-time at a language school of your choice, you can study English in the morning and work freely part-time in the afternoon and on weekends.</p>

<p><strong>Postgraduate (Master's) Degree Programs:</strong> While it is practically impossible to complete a 3-year Bachelor's or a 3-4 year PhD program within the 2-year YMS limit, you can successfully complete a UK Master's degree, which typically takes only 1 year. Whereas standard Student Visas restrict you from working more than 20 hours a week during term time, YMS visa holders have no restrictions on working hours, allowing you to seamlessly study for your Master's while working part-time or full-time.</p>

<p>Additionally, you are free to enroll in various paid community courses run by local councils in London, such as cooking, art, foreign languages, or vocational training courses.</p>
