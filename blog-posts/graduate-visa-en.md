<p>If you are currently studying at a UK university — or planning to do so — it is important to start thinking about what comes after graduation. Will you return home immediately once your studies are complete, or would you like to gain some work experience in the UK first? These are questions worth considering well in advance. For master's students, graduation may be just around the corner, and time passes quickly. Plan ahead so you are not caught off guard.</p><img class="blog-body-image" alt="UK Graduate Visa" src="/images/698104726ae5c16262bb97e8_Imin2UK-home-service-7-1024x683.webp" width="auto" height="auto" loading="auto"><h2>What Is the Graduate Visa?</h2><p>The UK Graduate Visa is a post-study work visa available to international students who have successfully completed a degree at a UK university. It allows you to live and work freely in the UK for a set period after graduation — 2 years for bachelor's and master's graduates, and 3 years for PhD graduates.</p><p><strong>Important notice:</strong> From 1 January 2027, bachelor's and master's graduates will receive <strong>18 months</strong> instead of 2 years under this route.</p><p>In some respects, the Graduate Visa resembles the Youth Mobility Scheme (YMS) in that it provides temporary permission to work in the UK. However, unlike the YMS, the Graduate Visa requires <strong>no financial evidence</strong> and is applied for from within the UK, making it a notably simpler process.</p><h2>When to Apply</h2><p>If you entered the UK on a Student visa for a master's programme, your visa will typically extend approximately 2 months beyond your course end date. For undergraduate or doctoral programmes lasting more than one year, you will generally receive around 4 months beyond your course end date. You should apply for the Graduate Visa within this 2- or 4-month window.</p><p>The reason you cannot apply earlier is that a key requirement of the Graduate Visa is proof that you have successfully completed your course of study. Once you have finished your final exam or submitted your final project, you must contact the relevant office at your university to confirm that your course completion has been reported to the Home Office.</p><h2>Important Notes on Course Completion</h2><p>It is important to understand that <strong>course completion is not the same as your graduation ceremony or the award of your degree certificate.</strong> Even if you have not yet received your certificate or attended graduation, your course may already be considered complete. The relevant point is when you have passed all required modules — including your dissertation or final project — and when that information has been notified to the Home Office.</p><p>When you contact your university, they may confirm this in writing, verbally, or by email. Whatever form the confirmation takes, make sure you receive it before proceeding with your application.</p><h2>How to Apply</h2><p>Once your university has confirmed that your course completion has been reported to the Home Office, you should apply without delay. There is no reason to wait, as the Graduate Visa must be applied for <strong>while your Student visa is still valid and while you are lawfully present in the UK.</strong></p><p>Apply online at <a href="https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa/apply" target="_blank">https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa/apply</a>. During the application, you will be asked to enter your CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) number — the reference issued by your university when your Student visa was granted. You do not need to submit separate documentation of your course completion; a declaration within the application form is sufficient.</p><p>If you are planning to visit Korea or travel in Europe after finishing your studies, make sure you apply for and receive your Graduate Visa before you travel.</p><h2>Application Fees</h2><p>Two fees are payable when submitting your online application.</p><p>First, the Graduate Visa application fee of £937. Second, the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) of £1,035 per year. This means £2,070 in total for a 2-year visa (bachelor's or master's), and £3,105 for a 3-year visa (PhD). The figures above are correct as of April 2026 and may be subject to further increases in future.</p><p>The IHS is higher than what you paid for your Student visa because the Graduate Visa is not eligible for the student discount — you are charged at the standard adult rate.</p><p>The easiest way to pay is using a UK bank debit card. If you are using a card issued overseas, make sure international payments are not blocked before you begin the application.</p><h2>Identity Verification</h2><p>Before starting your online application, install the UK Visa and Immigration ID Check app on your smartphone. You will use it to scan your passport and verify your identity. If the app does not work for you, you will need to book and attend an appointment at a UKVCAS service point in person. Try the app route first, as it is quicker and more convenient.</p><p>Additionally, if you received your Student visa before July 2024, you will have a BRP card — make sure it is to hand. If you received your Student visa more recently, you will have an eVisa, so generate a share code in advance.</p><h2>Dependants</h2><p>If your family members are currently in the UK as your dependants under your Student visa, they will each need to apply separately as dependants of your Graduate Visa. You will indicate within your own online application that you have dependants, and each family member must submit their own individual application along with supporting evidence of your relationship. The visa application fee and IHS must be paid separately for each person.</p><h2>Scholarship Recipients</h2><p>If your studies in the UK were funded by a scholarship, you should check the terms and conditions of your award carefully. Some scholarships include a requirement to return to your home country upon completion of your studies. Even if no such condition is stated, it is advisable to notify your scholarship body of your intention to apply for a Graduate Visa and seek their written consent. This consent letter should be submitted as part of your visa application.</p><h2>Processing Time</h2><p>Once submitted, your application may take up to two months to be decided. This is why it is strongly recommended to apply as soon as your course completion has been confirmed — do not leave it to the last minute.</p><p>If you submit your Graduate Visa application before your Student visa expires, you will not be considered to be overstaying, even if your Student visa expires while your application is still pending. If your Graduate Visa is refused, you must leave the UK immediately. If it is approved, you will be authorised to live and work in the UK from the date of the decision.</p><h2>Application Checklist</h2><p>Here is a summary of the key steps to prepare for your Graduate Visa application:</p><p>1. Confirm with your university that your course completion has been reported to the Home Office<br>2. Prepare your payment method — visa fee £937 + IHS £2,070 (2 years) or £3,105 (3 years)<br>3. BRP card holders: have your card ready / eVisa holders: generate a share code<br>4. Install the UK Visa and Immigration ID Check app on your smartphone (if not possible, book a UKVCAS appointment)<br>5. If you have dependants, prepare supporting evidence of family relationships</p><hr><p>If you are currently studying at a UK university, we strongly encourage you to apply for the Graduate Visa when you finish your course. You have invested significantly in your education here — gaining professional experience in the UK can add real value to your career. Whether you use the visa to build your CV over two or three years, transition to a Skilled Worker visa, or use your UK experience as a springboard for opportunities elsewhere in Europe, the Graduate Visa opens doors worth considering.</p><p>The Graduate Visa is one of the more straightforward UK visa categories — no financial evidence is required, and the application process is relatively simple. With careful preparation and timely action, most applicants can handle the process on their own.
