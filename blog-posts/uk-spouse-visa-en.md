<p>If your partner is a British citizen or holds Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) in the UK, and you wish to live together in the UK as a couple, the <strong>UK Spouse Visa</strong> is the route you need. This guide covers everything from basic eligibility and relationship requirements to financial thresholds, English language tests, application costs, and the eventual path to ILR — all in one place.</p>

<img class="blog-body-image" alt="UK Spouse Visa Complete Guide" src="/images/imin2uk-uk-spouse-visa.webp" width="auto" height="auto" loading="lazy">

<h2>What Is the UK Spouse Visa?</h2>

<p>The UK Spouse Visa is a visa applied for by someone who wishes to live in the UK with a partner who has the legal right to reside there. Its official basis is <strong>Appendix FM</strong> of the UK Immigration Rules, which covers spouses and partners. Importantly, this route is not limited to legally married couples. The following categories of applicants are all eligible:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Legally married spouses</li>
  <li>Civil partnership partners</li>
  <li>Fiancé(e)s (subject to specific conditions)</li>
  <li>Unmarried partners who have cohabited for at least 2 years</li>
</ul>

<p>In terms of structure, the person wishing to come to the UK is the <strong>applicant</strong>, while their UK-based partner — the British citizen or ILR holder — acts as the <strong>sponsor</strong>.</p>

<h2>Basic Eligibility Requirements</h2>

<p>Before looking at the specific conditions by relationship type, there are fundamental requirements that both parties must meet:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Both the applicant and the sponsor must be <strong>aged 18 or over</strong>.</li>
  <li>The sponsor must be a <strong>British citizen, Irish citizen, or hold ILR</strong> in the UK.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Requirements by Relationship Type</h2>

<h3>1. Married Couples and Civil Partners</h3>

<p>You must demonstrate that your marriage or civil partnership is legally recognised in the UK. For those who registered their marriage in South Korea, this can be evidenced by providing a Korean marriage certificate or its official English translation.</p>

<h3>2. Unmarried Cohabiting Partners</h3>

<p>If you are in an unmarried partnership — for example, living together with a British person in the US, Australia, or another country without having formally married — you must prove that you have been <strong>living together continuously for at least 2 years</strong> prior to the date of application. Acceptable evidence includes utility bills, tenancy agreements, council tax documents, and bank-related correspondence showing a shared address.</p>

<h3>3. Fiancé(e) Visa</h3>

<p>The Fiancé(e) Visa is a slightly special case. It grants entry to the UK, but you must <strong>complete a marriage or civil partnership within 6 months</strong> of arriving. Once married, you must then switch to a formal Spouse Visa from within the UK. Note that <strong>working and studying are not permitted</strong> during the Fiancé(e) Visa period.</p>

<h3>Common Requirements for All Relationship Types</h3>

<p>Regardless of which category you fall into, all applicants must satisfy the following conditions:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The couple must have <strong>met in person</strong>.</li>
  <li>The relationship must be <strong>genuine and subsisting</strong>.</li>
  <li>Any previous marriages or civil partnerships must have been <strong>legally dissolved</strong>.</li>
  <li>Both parties must intend to <strong>live together permanently in the UK</strong>.</li>
  <li>The marriage or civil partnership must be <strong>valid under the laws of the country</strong> in which it was formed.</li>
  <li>The couple must not be in a <strong>prohibited degree of relationship</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Financial Requirement</h2>

<p>The financial requirement is one of the most critical aspects of the Spouse Visa assessment. As of June 2026, the sponsor (the British or ILR-holding partner) must demonstrate a gross annual income of at least <strong>£29,000</strong>. However, how this is assessed varies significantly depending on your individual circumstances.</p>

<h3>Scenario 1: Both Partners Already Living in the UK</h3>

<p>If the applicant is already in the UK — for instance, on a Youth Mobility Scheme (YMS) visa or a Graduate Visa — and has married their British partner while in the UK, then the <strong>combined UK income of both partners</strong> can be used to meet the £29,000 threshold.</p>

<h3>Scenario 2: Currently Living Outside the UK (e.g., South Korea)</h3>

<p>If you and your British partner are currently living outside the UK, the rules are stricter. <strong>The applicant's overseas income cannot be used</strong> towards the financial requirement. Instead, you must evidence the sponsor's overseas income alongside their <strong>future UK income</strong> upon return.</p>

<p>For example, if the British partner is about to return to the UK and has already signed an employment contract with a UK employer offering at least £29,000 per year, this future UK income can be evidenced — provided it will commence within 3 months of returning. However, this alone is not sufficient; the couple must also demonstrate that the sponsor is currently earning £29,000 or more in the country of current residence (e.g., South Korea).</p>

<p>If obtaining a UK job offer in advance is not feasible, a practical alternative is for the <strong>British partner to return to the UK first</strong>, secure employment meeting the £29,000 threshold, and then have the applicant submit the Spouse Visa application from South Korea using that UK salary as evidence before travelling to the UK to join them.</p>

<h3>Meeting the Requirement Through Savings</h3>

<p>If it is genuinely not possible to meet the £29,000 income requirement, it can be substituted — in whole or in part — using <strong>savings held by either partner</strong>. The rules are as follows:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A minimum combined savings balance of <strong>£16,000</strong> is required.</li>
  <li>For any shortfall below the £29,000 income requirement, <strong>2.5 times the shortfall amount</strong> must be held in savings.</li>
  <li>In an extreme case where there is <strong>zero income</strong>, the required savings amount would be: £16,000 + (£29,000 × 2.5) = <strong>£88,500</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<p>Savings can be held in a financial institution in any country, including the UK or South Korea. However, there are important conditions:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Funds must be held as <strong>immediately accessible cash</strong>.</li>
  <li>The balance must have been maintained for a <strong>continuous period of at least 6 months</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Stocks, bonds, cryptocurrency, pension savings, and overdraft facilities</strong> are not accepted.</li>
</ul>

<p>In South Korea, many products named "pension savings" (연금저축) can actually be surrendered before retirement age to release cash. If this applies to you, it is advisable to surrender such products in advance and hold the resulting cash in an accessible account for the required 6-month period. The same applies to stocks, bonds, and cryptocurrency — these should be liquidated and held as cash for at least 6 months before the application.</p>

<h3>Welfare Benefit Exception</h3>

<p>If the British sponsor is receiving certain UK welfare benefits such as <strong>DLA (Disability Living Allowance)</strong> or <strong>PIP (Personal Independence Payment)</strong>, the £29,000 income requirement may be waived. However, even in this case, the couple must still demonstrate that they are financially self-sufficient to a reasonable degree.</p>

<h2>English Language Requirement</h2>

<p>The English language standard required varies depending on the stage of your immigration journey:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Initial application:</strong> CEFR <strong>A1</strong> level (basic speaking and listening)</li>
  <li><strong>First extension (after 30 months):</strong> CEFR <strong>A2</strong> level</li>
  <li><strong>ILR application (before 26 March 2027):</strong> CEFR <strong>B1</strong> level</li>
  <li><strong>ILR application (from 26 March 2027 onwards):</strong> CEFR <strong>B2</strong> level</li>
</ul>

<p>As you can see, the English language standard will progressively increase over time. There are two main ways to evidence this requirement:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A degree-level qualification taught in English at a UK educational institution; or, for qualifications from non-UK institutions, an <strong>Ecctis</strong> confirmation that the qualification was taught in English.</li>
  <li>A pass in a government-approved <strong>SELT (Secure English Language Test)</strong>, taken within <strong>2 years</strong> of the application date.</li>
</ul>

<p>The English language requirement is <strong>waived</strong> in the following cases:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Applicants aged <strong>65 or over</strong></li>
  <li>Those who are unable to meet the requirement due to a <strong>physical or mental condition</strong></li>
  <li>Nationals of <strong>majority English-speaking countries</strong> such as Australia, the USA, Canada, New Zealand, and Malta</li>
</ul>

<h2>Accommodation Requirement</h2>

<p>At the time of application, the couple must demonstrate that they have adequate accommodation in the UK that:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Does not rely on <strong>public funds</strong></li>
  <li>Is <strong>owned or exclusively occupied</strong> by the family</li>
  <li>Is <strong>large enough</strong> for the entire family included in the application</li>
  <li>Does <strong>not breach overcrowding regulations</strong> or public health rules</li>
</ul>

<p>For most couples who already have their own independent living space, this requirement will not present any significant difficulty.</p>

<p>However, if you are currently living outside the UK and the couple does not yet have accommodation arranged in the UK, you have two options. The first is to arrange a UK address before submitting the application. The second is to use the UK address of the sponsor's parents or relatives as a <strong>temporary address</strong>. In this case, additional evidence will likely be required — such as proof that an independent area of the property is available for the couple's exclusive use, and written permission from the property owner to occupy it.</p>

<h2>Application Costs</h2>

<p>The Spouse Visa carries relatively higher fees compared to many other immigration routes. As of June 2026, the costs are as follows:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Application from outside the UK</strong> (e.g., South Korea): <strong>£2,064</strong></li>
  <li><strong>In-country switching application</strong>: <strong>£1,407</strong></li>
  <li><strong>Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS)</strong>: <strong>£1,035 per year</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>The IHS is paid upfront for the full duration of the visa at the time of application, so please factor this into your overall budget.</p>

<h2>Visa Duration, Extension, and the Path to ILR</h2>

<h3>Initial Grant and Extensions</h3>

<p>The duration of the visa granted depends on where the application is submitted:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Applied from outside the UK:</strong> Initially granted up to <strong>2 years 9 months</strong>, then extended in <strong>2-year 6-month increments</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>In-country switching application:</strong> Initially granted <strong>2 years 6 months</strong>, then extended in <strong>2-year 6-month increments</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<p>Once the Spouse Visa is approved, the holder is <strong>permitted to work and study</strong> without restriction.</p>

<h3>Fiancé(e) Visa Exception</h3>

<p>The Fiancé(e) Visa grants a maximum stay of only <strong>6 months</strong>. Within that period, the couple must complete the marriage or civil partnership and submit an in-country switching application for a formal Spouse Visa. As mentioned above, <strong>working and studying are not permitted</strong> during this time.</p>

<h3>Path to Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)</h3>

<p>Under current immigration rules (as of 2026), Spouse Visa holders must have been continuously resident in the UK for a minimum of <strong>5 years</strong> before they are eligible to apply for ILR. In practice, if you have extended the Spouse Visa once, you will typically have accrued 5 years of continuous residence.</p>

<p>The ILR application for Spouse Visa holders requires a similar financial requirement to be met, alongside other qualifying conditions. If all requirements are satisfied, you may apply for <strong>UK Indefinite Leave to Remain after 5 years</strong>.</p>
