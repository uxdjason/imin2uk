<p>If you are already running a business in your home country and are considering expanding it into the UK while relocating yourself and your family, the <strong>GBM UK Expansion Worker</strong> route could be the most suitable immigration pathway for you. In this guide, we will walk you through everything you need to know — from the sponsor licence application to the visa requirements and your long-term settlement strategy.</p>

<h2>What Is the GBM UK Expansion Worker Route?</h2>

<p>The <strong>Global Business Mobility (GBM) UK Expansion Worker</strong> route is a UK immigration pathway designed for overseas businesses that wish to establish a new branch or subsidiary in the UK. It allows companies based outside the UK to send senior managers or key personnel to the UK to set up and develop their UK operations.</p>

<p>As the name suggests, this route is exclusively for companies that have <strong>not yet commenced trading in the UK</strong>. If a company is already actively operating in the UK, it is not eligible for this route.</p>

<p>Previously known as the "Sole Representative" visa, this pathway was restructured in 2022 under the umbrella of Global Business Mobility, becoming one of its sub-routes as the UK Expansion Worker.</p>

<h2>Sponsor Licence Requirements for the UK Expansion Worker Route</h2>

<p>Before deciding who to send to the UK, the first critical step is obtaining a <strong>UK Expansion Worker sponsor licence</strong> from the Home Office for the UK entity you are setting up. Regardless of whether you plan to send existing employees, hire locally, or come to the UK yourself as the company director, any business wishing to employ non-settled workers under this route must hold a valid sponsor licence.</p>

<p>There are three core requirements the overseas parent company must demonstrate:</p>

<h3>1. UK Footprint</h3>

<p>The overseas company must have already taken concrete steps to establish a presence in the UK. This can be evidenced by registering a UK company with Companies House, or by submitting a commercial property lease agreement for a UK business premises. Without satisfying the UK Footprint requirement, a sponsor licence application will be automatically refused.</p>

<h3>2. Minimum Three Years of Active Trading</h3>

<p>The overseas parent company must be able to demonstrate that it has been actively trading for <strong>at least three years</strong> prior to the date of application. Newly established businesses with less than three years of trading history are generally ineligible. Evidence such as corporate bank statements, financial statements, and audit records can be used to substantiate this.</p>

<p>If the parent company has been trading for less than three years but has a strong track record — for example, over one and a half to two years of demonstrably successful operations — an exception can be requested by making a compelling case. However, if approved, the licence will be restricted to sponsoring <strong>only one worker</strong>, meaning only one person (typically the director or head of the UK subsidiary) may be brought to the UK under the licence.</p>

<h3>3. A Credible UK Business Plan</h3>

<p>The Home Office must be satisfied that the company has a realistic and credible plan to build a genuine business in the UK — one capable of generating taxable revenue. A comprehensive business plan should include the parent company's trading history, any experience in other overseas markets, capital and investment details, and convincing market analysis and growth projections for the UK.</p>

<p>Importantly, the UK business plan must be in <strong>the same or a closely related sector</strong> as the overseas parent company's existing business. For instance, if the parent company operates as a web design and software agency, the UK expansion must also be in that field or an adjacent one. Pivoting to an entirely unrelated industry — such as a Korean cosmetics wholesale business when the parent company is a tech agency — would not be accepted.</p>

<p>The business plan must also be structured around a <strong>two-year timeframe</strong>, as the UK Expansion Worker route is designed to transition into a Skilled Worker sponsor licence within two years. Failure to establish genuine UK trading operations within this period can result in serious consequences: the company may lose the ability to sponsor new workers, have extensions refused for existing sponsored workers, or even have its licence revoked entirely.</p>

<p>Please also note that <strong>investment-focused businesses</strong> — such as private equity funds or venture capital firms — are not eligible to apply for a UK Expansion Worker sponsor licence.</p>

<h2>Practical Aspects of the Sponsor Licence</h2>

<p>Once the sponsor licence is granted, the company must designate an <strong>Authorising Officer (AO)</strong>, who holds overall responsibility for the licence. Typically, the person taking on the role of director or head of the UK subsidiary assumes this position.</p>

<p>In many cases — for example, where the overseas company's director or a senior executive is coming to the UK to lead the subsidiary — the AO will not yet be physically present in the UK at the time of the licence application. In this situation, the licence initially allows just <strong>one Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)</strong> to be issued: the AO's own CoS. Uniquely under this route, the AO is permitted to issue a CoS to themselves, which is then used to apply for the UK Expansion Worker visa.</p>

<p>Once the AO has arrived in the UK and updates their address and immigration status in the Sponsorship Management System (SMS), the company's rating is upgraded, allowing up to <strong>nine additional CoS allocations</strong> — bringing the total to a maximum of <strong>10 sponsored workers</strong> under the UK Expansion Worker licence.</p>

<p>It is important to clarify that this cap of 10 applies only to workers sponsored under the UK Expansion Worker licence. The company can freely employ British and Irish citizens, settled residents, and those who already hold visas that do not require sponsorship — such as the <strong>Youth Mobility Scheme (YMS)</strong> or <strong>Graduate Visa</strong> — without using any CoS allocation.</p>

<p>The UK Expansion Worker sponsor licence is classified under the <strong>Temporary Worker</strong> category, with a maximum validity of <strong>four years and no renewal</strong>. The four-year validity accounts for the possibility that some workers may be sponsored in the latter stages of the second year. One particularly cost-effective advantage of this route is that <strong>no Immigration Skills Charge (ISC)</strong> is required when issuing a CoS for UK Expansion Workers — a significant saving compared to the Skilled Worker route.</p>

<h2>UK Expansion Worker Visa Requirements</h2>

<p>Before going into the details, there is one crucial point to understand: the <strong>GBM UK Expansion Worker visa does not lead directly to Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)</strong>. As a Temporary Worker visa, it cannot be extended indefinitely, and time spent on this visa does not count towards the continuous residence period required for ILR. Those who wish to settle permanently in the UK must switch to an eligible route — most commonly the <strong>Skilled Worker visa</strong> — once the UK business is established.</p>

<p>The visa requirements are as follows:</p>

<h3>1. Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)</h3>

<p>The UK entity must issue a valid CoS under the UK Expansion Worker sponsor licence.</p>

<h3>2. Minimum One Year of Employment with the Overseas Parent Company</h3>

<p>The applicant must have been employed by the overseas parent company for <strong>at least 12 months</strong> (cumulative, not necessarily continuous) prior to the visa application. This must be evidenced through employment letters, payslips, employment contracts, and tax records. This requirement is waived if the salary offered by the UK entity is <strong>£73,900 or above</strong>.</p>

<h3>3. Currently Employed by the Overseas Parent Company</h3>

<p>The applicant must be <strong>currently employed</strong> by the overseas parent company at the time of application — it is not sufficient to have worked there previously.</p>

<h3>4. High-Skilled Occupation (RQF Level 6 or Above)</h3>

<p>The role to be performed in the UK must be at <strong>RQF Level 6 or above</strong> — that is, a high-skilled position such as a director, senior executive, or specialist technical role. Roles classified as medium-skilled or below are not eligible under this route.</p>

<h3>5. Minimum Salary of £52,500</h3>

<p>The salary offered by the UK entity must be at least <strong>£52,500 per annum</strong>. However, it is important to note that this minimum threshold is not the only consideration — the <strong>going rate</strong> for the specific occupation must also be met. If the going rate for the role exceeds £52,500, the higher amount applies.</p>

<h2>Visa Duration, Family, and English Language</h2>

<p>The UK Expansion Worker visa is initially granted for <strong>one year</strong>, with the option to extend for a further year — giving a maximum stay of <strong>two years</strong>. After this period, the visa holder must either switch to another immigration route or leave the UK.</p>

<p>Family members can accompany the visa holder to the UK. Dependants (spouse/partner and children) can be included in the application from the outset, provided the necessary financial requirements are met for each person. However, it is important to note that the company cannot cover the financial evidence on behalf of the applicant or their family members — personal funds must be demonstrated. Visa application fees and the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) also apply per person.</p>

<p>One notably favourable aspect of this route is that there is <strong>no English language requirement</strong>. Unlike many other UK immigration routes, applicants do not need to submit IELTS scores or evidence of a UK degree.</p>

<h2>Long-Term Strategy: From UK Expansion Worker to ILR</h2>

<p>If the UK business is successfully established within the two-year period, the company should transition to a <strong>Skilled Worker sponsor licence</strong>. At this point, the individual — whether they are the company director or a senior employee — can be employed under the Skilled Worker route, which does count towards the continuous residence period required for ILR. Alternatively, the individual could move to a Skilled Worker sponsor at another UK employer.</p>

<p>It is worth comparing this route to a direct Skilled Worker self-sponsorship approach. Some business owners ask whether it might save time and money to apply for a Skilled Worker licence from the outset. However, the two years spent operating under the UK Expansion Worker route builds a genuine and verifiable UK trading history — which becomes the foundation for a successful Skilled Worker licence application. Attempting to convince the Home Office of a viable UK business without any established local trading presence is far more difficult. We recommend following the phased approach encouraged by the Home Office rather than rushing into decisions that carry greater risk.</p>
