<p>Many people dream of building a new life and business in the UK. With the successive abolition of the Tier 1 Investor Visa and the Tier 1 Entrepreneur Visa, it is easy to assume that the barriers to UK business immigration have become insurmountably high. However, as of 2026, there are still legitimate and effective routes into the UK through business — provided you prepare thoroughly.</p>

<p>In this guide, we will walk you through three main pathways for those looking to enter the UK through their own business: the <strong>GBM UK Expansion Worker</strong> route for established Korean companies expanding into the UK, the <strong>Self-Sponsorship</strong> methodology for those looking to set up a new business in the UK, and the <strong>Innovator Founder Visa</strong> for those with a truly original business idea. We will also cover how each route leads to permanent residence so that you and your family can settle in the UK for the long term.</p>

<h2>Route 1: GBM UK Expansion Worker</h2>

<p>The <strong>UK Expansion Worker</strong> visa sits under the Global Business Mobility (GBM) category. In simple terms, this route is designed for businesses already operating successfully in Korea that wish to establish a branch or subsidiary in the UK and send over a senior employee or director to set it up. It was previously known as the Sole Representative Visa before being reclassified under the GBM framework.</p>

<h3>Who Is This Route For?</h3>

<p>This is the most suitable pathway for those who are already running an active business in Korea and are looking to use that business as a springboard into the UK market. The key requirements are as follows:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The Korean parent company must have been in active operation for <strong>at least 3 years</strong>.</li>
  <li>The UK entity must not yet have commenced active trading — a basic UK footprint such as a registered address or leased premises is acceptable, but the business must not already be trading in the UK.</li>
  <li>The Korean parent company does not need to be a limited company (corporations are not strictly required), though the UK entity being established <strong>must</strong> take the form of a legal corporate entity.</li>
  <li>The employee being sent to the UK must have worked at the Korean parent company for <strong>at least 12 months</strong>.</li>
  <li>The salary offered in the UK must be at least <strong>£52,500 per year</strong> or the going rate for the relevant occupation, whichever is higher.</li>
</ul>

<p>Notably, the director or owner of the Korean parent company can sponsor themselves as the overseas assignee, enabling the whole family to relocate to the UK together.</p>

<h3>Important Conditions to Be Aware Of</h3>

<p>Because this route is specifically designed for the purpose of establishing a new branch, the Korean parent company must continue to operate throughout. If the director themselves relocates to the UK, the Korean business must either be managed remotely or have staff in place to ensure its continued operation.</p>

<h3>Visa Duration and the Path to Permanent Residence</h3>

<p>The UK Expansion Worker visa is initially granted for <strong>1 year</strong>, with a possible extension of a further year — giving a maximum stay of <strong>2 years</strong> under this route. It does not lead directly to indefinite leave to remain (ILR).</p>

<p>Within those 2 years, the goal is to successfully establish the UK branch, after which the company must obtain a <strong>Skilled Worker Sponsor Licence</strong> and transition the director's visa to a <strong>Skilled Worker Visa</strong>. With 5 additional years on the Skilled Worker route, the total minimum path to ILR is approximately <strong>7 years</strong> of continuous UK residence.</p>

<p>In summary, the GBM UK Expansion Worker route is ideal for those who already run a stable, established business in Korea and are ready to make a strategic move into the UK market.</p>

<h2>Route 2: Self-Sponsorship (Skilled Worker via Your Own UK Company)</h2>

<p>Enquiries about <strong>Self-Sponsorship</strong> are among the most common we receive. To be precise, there is no such thing as a "Self-Sponsorship Visa" in UK immigration law. If you have heard of someone obtaining a Self-Sponsorship Visa, or seen an agency advertising one as a standalone product, it is likely a misrepresentation or misunderstanding.</p>

<p>What "Self-Sponsorship" actually refers to is an informal term — or a consulting package name — used to describe a specific strategy that combines two distinct processes: applying for a <strong>Skilled Worker Sponsor Licence</strong> and applying for a <strong>Skilled Worker Visa</strong>. The concept involves setting up your own UK company, having that company obtain a Sponsor Licence, and then sponsoring yourself as a Skilled Worker employee.</p>

<h3>How Does It Work?</h3>

<p>The structure works as follows:</p>

<ol>
  <li>You incorporate a new UK limited company (or acquire an existing one) in your name from Korea.</li>
  <li>That company applies to the Home Office for a <strong>Skilled Worker Sponsor Licence</strong>. At this stage, you must designate a legally resident <strong>Authorising Officer (AO)</strong> and <strong>Level 1 User</strong> based in the UK, and demonstrate a fully compliant HR system.</li>
  <li>Once the Sponsor Licence is approved, the company issues a <strong>Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)</strong> to you personally.</li>
  <li>You use that CoS to apply for your <strong>Skilled Worker Visa</strong> and enter the UK. Your dependant family members can accompany you.</li>
</ol>

<p>Under the current rules, after <strong>5 years</strong> of continuous residence on the Skilled Worker route, both you and your family members are eligible to apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR).</p>

<h3>Key Challenges and Requirements</h3>

<p>In recent years, the UK has significantly tightened its immigration policy, restricting Skilled Worker sponsorship to <strong>High Skilled occupations only</strong>. Medium Skilled roles are no longer eligible for new Skilled Worker applications or CoS issuance. This means that when a company applies for a Sponsor Licence, it must demonstrate that the roles it intends to sponsor fall within the High Skilled category.</p>

<p>To make this route viable, you will generally need to meet the following conditions:</p>

<ul>
  <li>A clearly defined and credible business plan that demonstrates the genuine substance of your UK operation.</li>
  <li>Sufficient capital — a minimum of approximately <strong>£100,000</strong>, or more safely around <strong>£150,000</strong>.</li>
  <li>The ability to hire yourself into a <strong>High Skilled occupation</strong> (such as Director or Company Executive) in a way that is logically consistent with your business model.</li>
  <li>A salary package that meets the required threshold for your occupation code.</li>
</ul>

<p>If your business plan lacks substance, your capital is insufficient, or your business type makes it implausible that you would be employed in a High Skilled role, the Sponsor Licence application itself is likely to be refused. This route is, therefore, not suitable for everyone — but for those with a solid business plan and the financial resources to back it up, it can be a highly effective path to UK immigration and permanent residence.</p>

<h2>Route 3: Innovator Founder Visa</h2>

<p>The final route we will cover is the <strong>Innovator Founder Visa</strong>, which replaced the former Innovator Visa and Start-up Visa. This pathway is designed for those who have a genuinely original business idea and wish to build a new venture in the UK from the ground up.</p>

<h3>The Endorsement Requirement</h3>

<p>The defining feature of this route is that you must first obtain an <strong>Endorsement Letter</strong> from a UK Home Office-approved <strong>Endorsing Body</strong>. Your business idea will be assessed against three criteria:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Innovative:</strong> Is your business idea genuinely new and distinct from what already exists in the market?</li>
  <li><strong>Viable:</strong> Is there a realistic and credible business model with genuine potential for revenue generation?</li>
  <li><strong>Scalable:</strong> Does the business have the potential to grow not only within the UK but also into EU and global markets?</li>
</ul>

<p>It is important to note that this visa applies only to <strong>new ventures</strong>. Joining or acquiring an existing trading business does not qualify.</p>

<h3>What Happens After Endorsement?</h3>

<p>Once your endorsement is secured, the visa application process itself is relatively straightforward. There is no minimum investment requirement and no restriction on business sector. You will simply need to provide evidence of English language proficiency and sufficient personal finances. Your dependant family members are also eligible to join you in the UK.</p>

<h3>The Fastest Route to UK Permanent Residence</h3>

<p>The most compelling advantage of the Innovator Founder route is the potential to achieve ILR in as little as <strong>3 years</strong>. After 3 years on this visa, if you can demonstrate sufficient business achievements, you can apply directly for Indefinite Leave to Remain. The qualifying criteria require you to meet at least <strong>2 of the 7 conditions</strong>. Below are the 3 conditions among the 7:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Securing investment of at least <strong>£50,000</strong></li>
  <li>Achieving annual revenue of at least <strong>£1,000,000</strong></li>
  <li>Creating at least <strong>10 jobs</strong> in the UK</li>
</ul>

<p>If you have a genuinely disruptive startup concept, advanced technical expertise, or specialist knowledge that could form the basis of a scalable business, the Innovator Founder Visa is well worth exploring.</p>

<h2>Choosing the Right Route for You</h2>

<p>To summarise the three routes covered in this guide:</p>

<ul>
  <li>If you already run an established Korean company and want to set up a UK branch, the <strong>GBM UK Expansion Worker</strong> route is your starting point.</li>
  <li>If you have substantial capital and a credible business plan to build a new UK company from scratch, the <strong>Self-Sponsorship methodology via Skilled Worker Visa</strong> may be the right path.</li>
  <li>If you have a truly innovative idea with global growth potential and want the fastest route to permanent residence, consider the <strong>Innovator Founder Visa</strong>.</li>
</ul>
